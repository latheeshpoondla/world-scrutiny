James Clear, author of the book Atomic Habits is an internationally renowned American writer. In this book Mr. Clear has given an important information about how small habits combinedly decide our life. The information provided by him was practically proved, which took place in the world around us, from his own life having a highly unforgettable memory in his childhood. Let’s look at the main points from his book. “**All the points are from the book**“.

1.  According to James Clear a habit is defined as a routine that is performed regularly – and, in many cases, automatically.

![](https://cdn.nextbigideaclub.com/wp-content/uploads/06205750/Screen-Shot-2018-09-28-at-2.54.53-PM.png)

3.  He wonderfully explained that habits often appear to make no difference until you cross a critical threshold and unlock a new level of performance. The impact created by change in your habits is similar to the effect of shifting the route of an airplane by just a few degrees.

> 4\. The most powerful outcomes are delayed.

5.  Habits need to persist long enough to break through this critical threshold – what James call the *Plateau of Latent Potential.* When you finally break through the Plateau of Latent Potential, people will call it an overnight success.
6.  True long-term thinking is a goal less thinking. It’s not about single accomplishment. It is about the cycle of endless refinement and continuous improvement. The most effective way to change your habits is to focus not on what you want to achieve, but on who you wish to become.

Every action is a vote for the type of person you wish to become. You become your habits.

7\. **The Habit Loop:**

1.  **Cue**
2.  **Carving**
3.  **Response**
4.  **Reward**

A cue is anything that triggers your brain to initiate a behavior. The cue triggers a craving, which motivates a response, which provides a reward, whish satisfies the craving and, ultimately, becomes associated with the cue. Together these four steps form a neurological feedback loop.

***Fact:***

1.  Snakes can smell by tasting the air with their highly sensitive tongues.
2.  Sharks can detect small amounts of electricity and vibrations in the water caused by nearby fish.

8\. Be the designer of your world and not merely the consumer of it. Make the cues of good habits obvious in your environment. Environment (Like your study environment – desk) design is powerful not only because it influences how we engage with the world but also because we rarely do it.

9\. Make it invisible – make the bad habit cues invisible. People with high self-control spend less time in temping situations. It’s easier to avoid temptation than resist it.

10\. Make it attractive – Make good habit cues more attractive than the bad ones. The more attractive an opportunity is, the more likely it is to become habit-forming.

11\. “ ***Lone Wolf dies, but the pack survives”*** – try to find the like-minded people instead of being alone. We can have good relationships if we manage to interactive with like-minded people for each and every habit. Ex: For habit 1, make some friends group 1 who has that habit and work together while doing it. For habit 2 make another group 2 of friends and work with them while doing it. but working with group 1 people for habit 2 is wrong thing, mostly you may not make much progress in it. And also, relationships may fall boring.

12\. Make it unattractive – Your current habits are not the necessarily the best way to solve the problems you face; they are just the methods you learned to use. Once you associate a solution with the problem you need to solve, you keep coming back to it. So, find more good solutions. Make the bad habits unattractive.

13\. Make it easy – When preparation becomes a form of procrastination, you need change something. You don’t want to merely be planning. you want to be practicing.

14\. ***You cannot simply remove bad habits, if you want to get rid of bad habits you have to replace it with good habits.***

15\. ***Redesign your life so the actions that matter most are also the actions that are easiest to do.***

16\. Instead of trying to engineer a perfect habit from the start, do the easy thing on a more consistent basis. ***“You have to standardize before you can optimize”.***

17\. Make it easy to start and rest will follow.

***18\. Make it satisfying – the best way to do this is to add a little bit of immediate pleasure to the habits that pay off in the long run and a little bit of immediate pain to ones that don’t.*** For you can also make a progress track, make note of every time you progress.

19\. Choose the habits that best suits you, not the one that is most popular. Habits need to be enjoyable if they are going to stick. This is the core idea behind the 4th law.

20\. For any habit or training at some point you will feel the boredom of doing every day, doing the same thing over and over again. The one who handles it will be the successful person.

21\. Habits are necessary, but not sufficient for mastery. What you need is a combination of automatic habits and deliberate practice.

“***Mastery is the process of narrowing your focus to a tiny element of success.***“

“***The hard and stiff will be broken. The soft and supple will prevail.***” – If you have made yourself a great identity in society. It may you to cling tighter to it. **The tighter we cling to an identity, the harder it becomes to grow beyond it**. We should never make any aspect of identity an overwhelming portion of who we are. We have to maintain a low profile, so that we may not neglect others opinions.

*It’s remarkable the business you can build if you don’t stop working consistentl*y. *It’s remarkable the body you can build if you don’t stop training. It’s remarkable the knowledge you can build if you don’t stop learning.*

Finally,

**Happiness is simply the absence of desire.**

I hope we all keep the important instructions from this book in our mind. Lead a good life.
